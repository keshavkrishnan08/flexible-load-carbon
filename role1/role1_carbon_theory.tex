\title[General Carbon Theory for Flexible Computing Loads]
{General Carbon Theory for Flexible Computing Loads:\\
The Capacity-Substitution Channel and a Distribution-Free\\
Net-Carbon-Reduction Condition}
\author{Team A1 --- Role 1 (General Carbon Theory)}
\date{Working results, Week~1}

\begin{abstract}
We harden and generalize the carbon analysis of the flexible-computing-load
(\textsc{fcl}) model beyond the uniform-distribution benchmark. Four results.
\emph{First}, we re-derive the uniform closed forms (paper eqs.\ 18--26) from
the first-order conditions and verify them numerically against direct
quadrature; along the way we establish the \emph{knife-edge} structure for the
entire power-law family and reconcile two transcription issues in the current
draft. \emph{Second}, we prove the central comparative-statics claim
$\KRs>\KRo$---renewable capacity is strictly higher with the \textsc{fcl}
program---for \emph{any} residual-demand distribution satisfying two conditions:
an economically transparent monotonicity of the \emph{shift ratio}
$g(x)=\FKR(x)/f(x)$, plus interior regularity. The program raises renewable
capacity whenever an extra megawatt of renewables relaxes residual demand more in
deep-surplus states than in tight states. The power-law family satisfies the
shift-ratio condition for every shape parameter, which is exactly why footnote~6's
extension goes through. \emph{Third}, we give the exact net-carbon inequality,
decompose it into a distribution-free \emph{utilization} effect and a
sign-controlled \emph{capacity-substitution} effect, and give a shape comparison to the
uniform at matched cutoffs (which bounds the saving but---as Week~2 shows the uniform's
interior value is negative---does not by itself sign interior carbon reduction).
\emph{Fourth}, we give a
distribution-free reading of the effect: \emph{at fixed equilibrium capacities} the
carbon change is affine in two conditional means, is bracketed by closed-form
\emph{magnitude bounds} computable from cutoff data, and---among distributions
sharing those capacities, within the natural shape class---is worst-cased by the
\emph{uniform}. Every analytical formula is verified against finite
differences of the profit integral to 6--16 digits. Of the points left open by the
uniform-only treatment, three are closed---the lattice (submodularity)
obstruction, the endogenous-capacity feedback (at fixed capacities), and the
renewable-cost threshold (general upper anchor; explicit window for the power-law
family)---and the regime map is unified up to the small-\textsc{fcl} crossover; a
companion memo keeps the same accounting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and notation}\label{sec:setup}

We work with the model of Section~3 of the paper and adopt its notation. A
regulated utility chooses renewable capacity $\KR$, dispatchable fossil backup
$\KF$, and flexible-computing-load capacity $\KM$ to maximize expected profit
over one representative period. The state variable is \emph{residual demand}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resdem}
  \tilde x \eqdef \tilde D - \KR\tilde S \in[-\KR,\Dbar],
\end{equation}
where $\tilde D\in[0,\Dbar]$ is stochastic consumer demand and
$\tilde S\in[0,1]$ the renewable capacity factor. Let $F(x;\KR)$ be the CDF of
$\tilde x$ with density $f(x;\KR)$, extended by $F\equiv0$ for $x<-\KR$ and
$F\equiv1$ for $x>\Dbar$. More renewables shift mass left:
$\FKR(x)\eqdef\partial F/\partial\KR\ge 0$ for every $x$.

Per-MWh quantities: \textsc{fcl} revenue $R$, fossil variable cost $c$, retail
price $p$, blackout penalty $\beta$. Amortized per-MW capital costs:
$\CR,\CF,\CM$. We maintain the paper's parameter regularity.

\begin{assumption}[Regularity]\label{ass:reg}
$R>c+\CM$,\quad $R<c+\CF+\CM$,\quad $\bp>c+\CF$,\quad $\bp>R$.
\end{assumption}

Under the optimal within-period dispatch rule
$d(x)=\min\{\max\{\KF-x,0\},\KM\}$ (paper Lemma~1), plugging into expected
profit and integrating by parts gives the unified profit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profit}
  \Pi(\KR,\KF,\KM)=p\,\E[\tilde D]
  +R\!\!\int_{\KF-\KM}^{\KF}\!\!\!F\,dx
  -c\!\!\int_{-\KM}^{\KF-\KM}\!\!\!(1-F)\,dx
  -(\bp)\!\!\int_{\KF}^{\Dbar}\!\!\!(1-F)\,dx
  -\CF\KF-\CR\KR-\CM\KM ,
\end{equation}
valid in both the large-\textsc{fcl} regime ($\KM\ge\KR$) and the
small-\textsc{fcl} regime ($\KM<\KR$) by the extended-CDF convention. Setting
$\KM=0$ gives the no-\textsc{fcl} benchmark $\Pi_0$.

\begin{lemma}[Unified first-order conditions]\label{lem:foc}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:profit} by Leibniz's rule,
\begin{align}
  \tfrac{\partial\Pi}{\partial\KM}
    &=(R-c)\,F(\KF-\KM;\KR)+c\,F(-\KM;\KR)-\CM,\label{eq:gM}\\[2pt]
  \tfrac{\partial\Pi}{\partial\KF}
    &=R\big[F(\KF)-F(\KF-\KM)\big]-c\big[1-F(\KF-\KM)\big]
      +(\bp)\big[1-F(\KF)\big]-\CF,\label{eq:gF}\\[2pt]
  \tfrac{\partial\Pi}{\partial\KR}
    &=R\!\!\int_{\KF-\KM}^{\KF}\!\!\FKR\,dx
      +c\!\!\int_{-\KM}^{\KF-\KM}\!\!\FKR\,dx
      +(\bp)\!\!\int_{\KF}^{\Dbar}\!\!\FKR\,dx-\CR.\label{eq:gR}
\end{align}
In the large-\textsc{fcl} regime $F(-\KM;\KR)=0$, and \eqref{eq:gM}--\eqref{eq:gR}
collapse to the paper's eqs.\ (12), (13), (15) respectively.\footnote{We verified
this collapse and the small-\textsc{fcl} (Appendix~G) limit numerically; the two
regimes share one formula because the extended CDF is already zero below $-\KR$.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Distribution-free equilibrium probabilities]\label{lem:cutoffs}
At any interior optimum, the no-\textsc{fcl} threshold satisfies
$F(\KFo;\KRo)=\varphi^{\circ}\eqdef\frac{\bp-c-\CF}{\bp-c}$, independent of the
shape of $F$. In the \emph{large-\textsc{fcl}} regime ($F(-\KMs;\KRs)=0$) the two
\textsc{fcl} thresholds are likewise distribution-free:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoffs}
  F(\KFs;\KRs)=\frac{\bp-c-\CF-\CM}{\bp-R},
  \qquad
  F(\KFs-\KMs;\KRs)=\frac{\CM}{R-c}.
\end{equation}
In the small-\textsc{fcl} regime the term $cF(-\KMs;\KRs)$ in \eqref{eq:gM} does
not vanish, and these two values are not pinned by the FOCs alone.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first identity is the no-\textsc{fcl} FOC \eqref{eq:gF} with $\KM=0$; the
other two solve \eqref{eq:gM}--\eqref{eq:gF}. Each fixes a \emph{value} of $F$,
not a capacity, so no distributional assumption enters. \emph{(Verified on the
non-uniform $\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$ scale-mixture: both equal $\tfrac{5}{9}$ and
$\tfrac{17}{18}$ to $10^{-9}$.)}
\end{proof}

\noindent These give the adoption threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cbar}
  \CMbar=(R-c)\,\varphi^{\circ}=\frac{(R-c)(\bp-c-\CF)}{\bp-c},
\end{equation}
the per-MW value of computing hardware equal to the margin $R-c$ times the
fraction of time spare fossil capacity is available. Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs} is
the lever that makes the carbon comparison of \S\ref{sec:carbon} general: the
program shifts the equilibrium \emph{capacities}, but the \emph{probabilities}
at which fossil cuts in and \textsc{fcl} saturates are pinned.

\section{Ground truth: the uniform closed form}\label{sec:uniform}

We re-derive the uniform benchmark used to sign the carbon result, both to lock
in correctness and to fix the algebraic constants the general theory must
reproduce. Throughout this section $F(x;\KR)=\dfrac{x+\KR}{\Dbar+\KR}$ on
$[-\KR,\Dbar]$, so $f=1/Q$ and $\FKR(x)=(\Dbar-x)/Q^2$ with $Q\eqdef\Dbar+\KR$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform optimal capacities]\label{lem:uniform}
Write $Q^{\circ}=\Dbar+\KRo$ and $Q^{*}=\Dbar+\KRs$. Then
\begin{align}
  \KFo&=\Dbar-\frac{\CF}{\bp-c}\,Q^{\circ},
  &\KRo&=\Dbar\!\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{c}{\,2\CR-\CF^{2}/(\bp-c)\,}}-1\right),
  \label{eq:noKFKR}\\[4pt]
  \KFs&=\Dbar+\frac{R-c-\CF-\CM}{\bp-R}\,Q^{*},
  &\KFs-\KMs&=\Dbar-\frac{R-\CM-c}{R-c}\,Q^{*}.
  \label{eq:fclKFKM}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\eqref{eq:fclKFKM} and the first identity in \eqref{eq:noKFKR} invert the cutoff
probabilities \eqref{eq:cutoffs} under the linear CDF. For $\KRo$, substitute
$\KFo$ into the renewable FOC \eqref{eq:gR} with $\KM=0$. Writing
$\gamma\eqdef\CF/(\bp-c)$ so that $\KFo=\Dbar-\gamma Q^{\circ}$ and
$\Dbar-\KFo=\gamma Q^{\circ}$, and using
$\int_a^b\FKR\,dx=\big[(b-a)(\Dbar-\tfrac{a+b}{2})\big]/Q^2$,
\[
  \underbrace{c\,\frac{\Dbar^2-\gamma^2 Q^{\circ2}}{2Q^{\circ2}}}_{\text{from }(0,\KFo)}
  +\underbrace{\frac{\bp}{2}\gamma^2}_{\text{from }(\KFo,\Dbar)}=\CR
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \frac{c\,\Dbar^2}{Q^{\circ 2}}=2\CR-\frac{\CF^2}{\bp-c},
\]
because $\gamma^2(\bp-c)=\CF^2/(\bp-c)$. Solving for $Q^{\circ}$ and subtracting
$\Dbar$ gives \eqref{eq:noKFKR}. \emph{(Verified against the quadrature solver: the
capacities satisfy the FOCs to $\le10^{-15}$.)}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Carbon (paper eqs.\ 25--26).}
Expected fossil output without the program is
$\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]=\KFo-\int_0^{\KFo}F\,dx
   =\KFo\big(\Dbar-\tfrac{\KFo}{2}\big)/Q^{\circ}$,
and with the program (large-\textsc{fcl} regime), accounting for the flat stretch
$(\KMs-\KRs)$ on which the extended CDF equals zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fosfcl}
  \E[\text{fossil}^{*}]
  =(\KMs-\KRs)+\int_{-\KRs}^{\KFs-\KMs}\!(1-F)\,dx
  =\Dbar+\frac{R-c-\CF-\CM}{\bp-R}\,Q^{*}-\frac12\Big(\frac{\CM}{R-c}\Big)^{2}Q^{*}.
\end{equation}
Carbon emissions equal $c$ times these quantities. Net carbon falls with the
program iff $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]<\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]$.

\begin{remark}[Two corrections to the current draft]\label{rem:corr}
\emph{(i)} The bracketed expressions printed in eqs.\ (25)--(26) are
\emph{expected fossil output} ($=\text{carbon}/c$), not carbon itself; the
constant $c$ is dropped because only the sign of (25)$-$(26) matters. Restoring
$c$, our \eqref{eq:fosfcl} reproduces the printed eq.\ (26) to $6\times10^{-10}$
and eq.\ (25) to machine precision.
\emph{(ii)} The naive ``small-\textsc{fcl}'' fossil formula
$\KF(\Dbar+\KM-\KF/2)/Q$ is invalid here: in the large-\textsc{fcl} regime the
integration range $[-\KM,\KF-\KM]$ protrudes below $-\KR$, where $1-F=1$
exactly. The flat term $(\KMs-\KRs)$ in \eqref{eq:fosfcl} is the correction.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Knife-edge for the power-law family]\label{prop:knife}
Let $F(x;\KR)=\big((x+\KR)/(\Dbar+\KR)\big)^{\alpha}$ on $[-\KR,\Dbar]$ for any
$\alpha>0$ (the uniform is $\alpha=1$). Along the locus where $\KF,\KM$ are
re-optimized at every $\KR$, the marginal value of renewable capacity is
\emph{constant in $\KR$}. In the interior regime it equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mv}
  \mathrm{MV}=\tfrac12\!\left[\,c
  +\frac{(R-\CM-c)^2}{R-c}
  +\frac{(R-c-\CF-\CM)^2}{\bp-R}\right],
\end{equation}
so $\KRs=\infty$ if $\mathrm{MV}>\CR$, $\KRs=0$ if $\mathrm{MV}<\CR$, and any
$\KR$ is optimal at equality. Equivalently $\KRs=\infty$ iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eq24}
  c+\frac{(R-\CM-c)^2}{R-c}+\frac{(R-c-\CF-\CM)^2}{\bp-R}>2\CR ,
\end{equation}
which is \emph{exactly} the bracket printed in paper eq.\ (24).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The $\KRs=\infty$ corner is a small-\textsc{fcl} statement]\label{rem:knifecaveat}
Formula \eqref{eq:mv} is the marginal value on the \emph{large}-\textsc{fcl} portion of
the re-optimization locus. Along that locus $\KMs/\KRs\to(1-\varphi_1)-\tfrac{c+\CF+\CM-R}{\bp-R}<1$,
so $\KMs<\KRs$ for large $\KR$: the locus \emph{leaves} the large-\textsc{fcl} regime before
$\KR=\infty$. The genuine $\KR\to\infty$ corner is therefore governed by the
small-\textsc{fcl} asymptotic slope (Appendix~G / Role~4), and it does \emph{not} agree
with \eqref{eq:mv}. Numerically, at the calibration of \S\ref{sec:num} the marginal value
is constant at $\mathrm{MV}=0.3444$ only while the locus remains large-\textsc{fcl}
($\KR\lesssim6.3$); past the regime crossing it declines strictly, to the asymptote
$\approx0.1322$. Hence
\[
  \CR^{\dagger}\;=\;\lim_{\KR\to\infty}\mathrm{MV}(\KR)\;\approx\;0.1322
  \qquad\text{not}\qquad \mathrm{MV}=0.3444 ,
\]
an overstatement by a factor of $\approx2.6$. The correct strict-fall window is therefore
$(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})\approx(0.0400,\,0.1322)$, not $(0.0400,\,0.3444)$: on
$(0.1322,0.3444)$ the renewable optimum is \emph{finite}, so $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]>0$ and
the corner argument does not apply. \textbf{The non-emptiness conclusion survives}---the
corrected window is still non-empty---but every quoted value of $\CR^{\dagger}$, and any
magnitude computed on the over-wide window, must use the small-\textsc{fcl} asymptote.
Downstream results that invoke the $\KRs=\infty$ corner inherit this correction; they are
proved here for the power/uniform families, with the general regime-stitching deferred to
Role~4.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
For the power law one computes $\FKR(x)=f(x)\,(\Dbar-x)/Q$, hence the shift ratio
$g(x)=\FKR/f=(\Dbar-x)/Q$ is the \emph{same} affine function for every $\alpha$.
Substituting the linear loci \eqref{eq:fclKFKM} into \eqref{eq:gR} and evaluating
$\int_a^b\FKR\,dx$ over the three regions (whose endpoints are all of the form
$\Dbar-(\,\cdot\,)Q$), every $Q$ cancels, leaving \eqref{eq:mv}; the marginal
return is constant in $\KR$, so the objective is linear along the locus and the
optimum is a corner. Multiplying \eqref{eq:mv} by $2$ and subtracting $2\CR$
gives precisely the paper's eq.\ (24) bracket, confirming
$\text{(eq.\ 24)}=2\,(\mathrm{MV}-\CR)=2\,\partial\Pi/\partial\KR$ along the
locus. \emph{(MV constant in $\KR$ verified at $\KR\in\{1,5,9\}$ for
$\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$.)}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[On eq.\ (24)]
Proposition~\ref{prop:knife} shows eq.\ (24) is \emph{correct as a sign
condition}: the printed bracket equals twice the profit gradient
$\partial\Pi/\partial\KR$, and only its sign is used (the $\infty/0$
trichotomy). The factor of two is a grouping artifact of the closed-form
substitution, not an error---worth a one-line clarifying footnote in the draft.
\end{remark}

\section{The capacity-substitution channel, in general}\label{sec:capacity}

The carbon result rests on a single comparative-statics claim that the paper
asserts but does not prove outside the uniform case: \emph{renewable capacity is
higher with the program}, $\KRs>\KRo$. We now prove it for any distribution
satisfying one transparent condition. The obstruction, and why the naive
lattice argument fails, is recorded in Section~\ref{sec:gap}.

\subsection{The marginal-value cross-partials}
From Lemma~\ref{lem:foc}, the second derivatives of $\Pi$ that govern how the
optimum moves when computing becomes more viable are
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial^2\Pi}{\partial\KR\,\partial\KM}
    &=(R-c)\,\FKR(\KF-\KM)+c\,\FKR(-\KM)\;\ge 0,\label{eq:hRM}\\
  \frac{\partial^2\Pi}{\partial\KF\,\partial\KM}
    &=(R-c)\,f(\KF-\KM)\;\ge 0,\label{eq:hFM}\\
  \frac{\partial^2\Pi}{\partial\KR\,\partial\KF}
    &=(R-\bp)\,\FKR(\KF)-(R-c)\,\FKR(\KF-\KM)\;\le 0.\label{eq:hRF}
\end{align}
So computing raises the marginal value of \emph{both} capacities
(\eqref{eq:hRM}--\eqref{eq:hFM} are nonnegative because $R>c$ and $\FKR\ge0$),
yet renewables and fossil are mutual substitutes in the objective
(\eqref{eq:hRF} is nonpositive because $\bp>R$). The sign conflict in
\eqref{eq:hRF} is exactly why a one-line Topkis argument does not deliver
$\KRs>\KRo$; see Section~\ref{sec:gap}.

\subsection{The shift-ratio condition}
Define the \emph{shift ratio}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
  g(x;\KR)\eqdef\frac{\FKR(x;\KR)}{f(x;\KR)}
  =-\left.\frac{\partial x}{\partial\KR}\right|_{F\,\text{fixed}} ,
\end{equation}
the megawatts by which one extra MW of renewables pulls down the residual-demand
level holding its quantile fixed---the strength of the renewable
\emph{leftward shift} at state $x$.

\begin{condition}[Shift ratio]\label{cond:cs}
$g(x;\KR)$ is non-increasing in $x$ on $(\KFs-\KMs,\,\KFs)$ at each point of the
equilibrium path $\CM\in(0,\CMbar]$.
\end{condition}

Condition~\ref{cond:cs} says the renewable shift bites harder in deep-surplus
states than in tight states. It is mild and interpretable, and it holds for the
entire power-law family, where $g(x)=(\Dbar-x)/Q$ is affine and strictly
decreasing for every $\alpha$.

\begin{condition}[Regularity]\label{cond:reg}
On the adoption path $\CM\in(0,\CMbar]$ the program has an interior maximizer
$(\KRs,\KFs,\KMs)$ at which the Hessian $\mathcal H$ of $\Pi$ is nonsingular.
\end{condition}

\noindent Condition~\ref{cond:reg} is exactly what the implicit function theorem
needs to make the path $\CM\mapsto(\KRs,\KFs,\KMs)$ a $C^1$ curve; at a maximizer,
nonsingularity forces $\mathcal H\prec0$. It is verified directly:
\S\ref{sec:num} computes the eigenvalues of $\mathcal H$ along the entire path and
finds all three strictly negative throughout the interior region. The one place it
fails is the \emph{knife-edge boundary}, where $\CR$ is small enough that
$\KRs\!\to\!\infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:knife}): there $\mathcal H$ acquires a
zero eigenvalue as the marginal value of renewables turns flat. But that boundary
needs no comparative-statics argument---$\KRs=\infty>\KRo$ holds trivially---so
Condition~\ref{cond:reg} costs nothing where Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} is actually
used (interior optima with finite $\KRs$).

\begin{theorem}[Capacity-substitution channel]\label{thm:capacity}
Under Conditions~\ref{cond:cs}--\ref{cond:reg}, at every interior point of the
adoption path the equilibrium derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dKRdCM}
  \frac{d\KRs}{d\CM}
  =\frac{\operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}(\mathcal H)}{\det\mathcal H}\;\le 0,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}(\mathcal H)\xrightarrow[\text{large-\textsc{fcl}}]{}
  (R-\bp)(R-c)\big[\FKR(\KFs)f(\KFs-\KMs)-\FKR(\KFs-\KMs)f(\KFs)\big],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H$ is the Hessian of $\Pi$ (so $\det\mathcal H<0$), and the
displayed bracket is the large-\textsc{fcl} ($\KMs>\KRs$) form of the cofactor.
Consequently, integrating from the adoption boundary $\CMbar$---where $\KMs=0$ and
the system collapses to the no-\textsc{fcl} optimum, $\KRs(\CMbar)=\KRo$---we
obtain
\begin{equation}
  \KRs(\CM)\;\ge\;\KRo\qquad\text{for all }\CM\le\CMbar,
\end{equation}
with strict inequality whenever $\CM<\CMbar$ and $g$ is strictly decreasing.
Renewable capacity is strictly higher with the \textsc{fcl} program.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only $\partial\Pi/\partial\KM$ in \eqref{eq:gM} depends on $\CM$, with
$\partial^2\Pi/\partial\KM\partial\CM=-1$. Implicit differentiation of the FOC
system $\nabla\Pi=0$ gives
$d\KRs/d\CM=(\mathcal H^{-1})_{\KR\KM}
   =\operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}(\mathcal H)/\det\mathcal H$.
In the large-\textsc{fcl} regime $\FKR(-\KM)=f(-\KM)=0$, so by
\eqref{eq:hRM}--\eqref{eq:hRF} and $\partial^2\Pi/\partial\KM^2=-(R-c)f(\KF-\KM)$,
the relevant cofactor simplifies (after cancellation) to
\[
  \operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}(\mathcal H)
  =(R-\bp)(R-c)\big[\FKR(\KFs)f(\KFs-\KMs)-\FKR(\KFs-\KMs)f(\KFs)\big].
\]
Dividing by $\det\mathcal H<0$ gives \eqref{eq:dKRdCM}. The bracket equals
$f(\KFs)f(\KFs-\KMs)\big[g(\KFs)-g(\KFs-\KMs)\big]\le 0$ under
Condition~\ref{cond:cs} (since $\KFs-\KMs<\KFs$); because $\bp>R$ we have
$(R-\bp)(R-c)<0$, so the cofactor is $\ge0$, and dividing by $\det\mathcal H<0$
gives $d\KRs/d\CM\le0$.

\emph{The path crosses regimes.} At $\CM=\CMbar$ the marginal profit of the first
\textsc{fcl} unit \eqref{eq:gM} vanishes ($(R-c)\varphi^{\circ}-\CMbar=0$), so
$\KMs=0$: the top of the path is the \emph{small}-\textsc{fcl} regime
($\KMs<\KRs$), turning large-\textsc{fcl} only once $\KMs$ grows past $\KRs$. In
the small-\textsc{fcl} regime $\FKR(-\KMs)>0$, which adds a single term to the
relevant cofactor $\operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}(\mathcal H)=H_{RF}H_{FM}-H_{RM}H_{FF}$
through $H_{RM}=(R-c)\FKR(\KFs-\KMs)+c\,\FKR(-\KMs)$: it becomes
$\operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}^{\text{large}}-c\,\FKR(-\KMs)\,H_{FF}$, and since
$H_{FF}=(R-\bp)f(\KFs)-(R-c)f(\KFs-\KMs)<0$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:reg} this
extra term is $\ge0$. Hence $\operatorname{cof}_{\KM\KR}\ge0$ and $d\KRs/d\CM\le0$
\emph{throughout} the path, not only on its large-\textsc{fcl} part (confirmed
numerically across the crossing in \S\ref{sec:num}: $d\KRs/d\CM<0$ at every
$\CM\in\{0.77,\dots,0.30\}$, spanning both regimes). Since the no-\textsc{fcl}
conditions hold at $\CM=\CMbar$, $\KRs(\CMbar)=\KRo$. By the implicit function
theorem (Condition~\ref{cond:reg}, $\mathcal H$ nonsingular), $\CM\mapsto\KRs$ is
$C^1$ on each of the two open regime intervals and continuous across the single
boundary point $\KMs=\KRs$; hence it is \emph{absolutely continuous} on the
compact interval $[\CM,\CMbar]$. An absolutely continuous function with
a.e.-nonpositive derivative is non-increasing, so integrating
$d\KRs/d\CM\le0$ from $\CMbar$ down to $\CM$ yields $\KRs(\CM)\ge\KRo$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The submodularity is not an obstruction]\label{rem:submod}
By \eqref{eq:hRF}, $\Pi$ is \emph{submodular} in $(\KR,\KF)$ while having
increasing differences in $(\KR;\KM)$ and $(\KF;\KM)$; since reversing the order
on $\KF$ flips the latter signs, no single relabeling restores supermodularity,
and Topkis/Milgrom--Shannon cannot be invoked off the shelf. Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}
shows this is immaterial: the homotopy in $\CM$ together with the local cofactor
sign is a \emph{complete} proof, requiring only Condition~\ref{cond:reg}. The
lattice machinery is sufficient, not necessary, for monotone comparative statics;
a signed one-parameter cofactor does the same job here without it.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Power-law / footnote~6]\label{cor:power}
For every power-law residual demand ($\alpha>0$), $g(x)=(\Dbar-x)/Q$ is strictly
decreasing, so Condition~\ref{cond:cs} holds and $\KRs>\KRo$ whenever
$\CM<\CMbar$. This is precisely why footnote~6's closed-form extension carries
through for the whole family rather than the uniform case alone.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Reading of Condition~\ref{cond:cs}]
Writing $\tilde x=\tilde D-\KR\tilde S$, the shift ratio is the
density-weighted conditional capacity factor,
$g(x)=\E\!\big[\tilde S \mid \text{quantile at }x\big]$ in the natural sense.
Condition~\ref{cond:cs} fails only if high-demand states are systematically
\emph{windier/sunnier} than surplus states---an empirically unusual positive
demand--supply correlation. Under the more realistic negative or zero
correlation, the condition holds and the channel operates.
\end{remark}

\section{The net carbon condition, in general}\label{sec:carbon}

Carbon emissions equal $c$ times expected fossil output, so the program lowers
carbon iff $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]<\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]$. Decompose
\eqref{eq:profit}'s fossil terms exactly. Let
$q^{\circ}\eqdef F(0;\KRo)$ be the no-\textsc{fcl} surplus probability and define
the conditional means
\[
  \mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\eqdef
  \frac{1}{F(\KFs-\KMs;\KRs)}\!\int_{-\KRs}^{\KFs-\KMs}\!\!x\,f(x;\KRs)\,dx,
  \qquad
  \mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}\eqdef
  \frac{1}{\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ}}\!\int_{0}^{\KFo}\!\!x\,f(x;\KRo)\,dx .
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact net-carbon inequality]\label{prop:carbon}
For any $F$ satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:reg},
\begin{align}
  \E[\text{fossil}^{*}] &=\KFs-\tfrac{\CM}{R-c}\big(\KFs-\KMs-\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\big),
  \label{eq:fos1}\\
  \E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}] &=\KFo(1-\varphi^{\circ})+(\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ})\,\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}},
  \label{eq:fos2}
\end{align}
and carbon falls with the program iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
  \underbrace{\frac{\CM}{R-c}\big(\KFs-\KMs-\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\big)}_{\text{utilization effect }(+)}
  \;>\;
  \underbrace{\KFs-\KFo(1-\varphi^{\circ})-(\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ})\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}}_{\text{net of capacity effects}}.
  \tag{C}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrate each fossil integral by parts using
$\int_a^b(1-F)\,dx=[x(1-F)]_a^b+\int_a^b x f\,dx$; for \eqref{eq:fos1} take
$a=-\KMs,\ b=\KFs-\KMs$ with $F(a;\KRs)=0$ (large-\textsc{fcl}) and
$F(b;\KRs)=\CM/(R-c)$; for \eqref{eq:fos2} take $a=0,\ b=\KFo$ with
$F(\KFo;\KRo)=\varphi^{\circ}$. Subtract.
\end{proof}

Two forces. The \textbf{utilization effect} (the left side of (C)) is
\emph{always positive and distribution-free}: at fixed capacities the program
strictly raises fossil output in every state below $\KF$ (Lemma~1 dispatch).
The \textbf{capacity-substitution effect} is carried by the right side through
$\KRs>\KRo$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}), which lowers $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]$ by
shifting the residual-demand distribution left. The net sign is which one wins.

\begin{corollary}[Shape comparison to the uniform, at matched cutoffs]\label{cor:suff}
For any $F$ with
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item $f(\cdot;\KRs)$ non-increasing on $(-\KRs,\KFs-\KMs)$ \;(equiv.\ $F(\cdot;\KRs)$ concave there), and
  \item $f(\cdot;\KRo)$ non-decreasing on $(0,\KFo)$ \;(equiv.\ $F(\cdot;\KRo)$ convex there),
\end{enumerate}
we have $\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\le(\KFs-\KMs-\KRs)/2$ (so \textsc{fcl} fossil output is no
larger than under the uniform) and $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge\KFo/2$ (so no-\textsc{fcl}
output is no smaller). Hence the net saving $\Delta$ is \emph{at least} its uniform value at
the same distribution-free cutoffs. \textbf{This is a shape comparison, not a proof of
carbon reduction.} It signs $\Delta>0$ only where the uniform benchmark already does---and
Week~2 shows the uniform's \emph{interior} value $\Delta_0<0$ (carbon falls under the
uniform only at its corner $\KRs\to\infty$, where $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]\to0$). So (a)--(b)
bound $\Delta$ from below but do \emph{not} by themselves establish interior carbon
reduction; that requires the optimized (surplus) contract of Week~3 or the corner analysis.
The uniform satisfies (a)--(b) with equality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why no level-feedback term is missing]\label{rem:feedback}
One might worry that (a)--(b) compare conditional means while the capacity
\emph{levels} $\KFs,\KRs$ themselves move with the shape of $F$. They do, but the
comparison is nonetheless exact, for two reasons. First, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs} the \emph{probabilities} at the comparison limits
($\CM/(R-c)$ at $\KFs-\KMs$, $\varphi^{\circ}$ at $\KFo$) are distribution-free,
so (a)--(b) bound genuine, like-for-like quantile integrals---there is no hidden
dependence on $F$ in the limits. Second, the one level effect that does survive,
the renewable shift $\KRs>\KRo$, is signed once and for all by
Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}; it enters \eqref{eq:fos1} only through the leftward
relocation of mass, which (a) already accounts for. Hence (C) together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} is an \emph{exact} identity at fixed cutoffs---no
second-order feedback term is dropped. (It is not a distribution-free \emph{sign}: Week~2
shows the sign of $\Delta$ across $F$ is not decidable from the cutoffs and capacities
alone.)
\end{remark}

Write $\Delta(\CR)\eqdef\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]-\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]$ for the
net fossil saving at the optimum given renewable cost $\CR$ (carbon falls iff
$\Delta>0$). The next theorem has a \emph{fully general} upper anchor and a
small-cost window established for the knife-edge (power-law / scale) families.

\begin{theorem}[Viability threshold: general anchor, explicit window]\label{thm:carbon}
Fix all primitives except $\CR$, with $\CM<\CMbar$ (so $\KMs>0$).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item \textup{(Large-cost anchor --- holds for every $F$.)} For all $\CR$ above a
        finite $B_0$, no renewables are built, $\KRs=\KRo=0$, and the \textsc{fcl}
        program differs from the no-\textsc{fcl} grid only by running computing on
        spare fossil; the utilization effect is strictly positive, so
        $\Delta(\CR)=-U_0<0$. Hence the carbon-reduction set $\{\CR:\Delta(\CR)\ge0\}$
        is bounded above, for \emph{every} $F$.
  \item \textup{(Small-cost window --- knife-edge families.)} Suppose the
        \textsc{fcl} renewable problem admits unbounded investment for small $\CR$
        (true for the power-law family, Proposition~\ref{prop:knife}, and more
        generally any family whose renewable marginal value is constant along the
        re-optimization locus), and that renewable output is a.s.\ positive. Then
        there are thresholds $0\le\CR^{\circ}<\CR^{\dagger}$ with: for
        $\CR<\CR^{\dagger}$, $\KRs=\infty$ and $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]=0$; for
        $\CR>\CR^{\circ}$, $\KRo$ finite and $\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]>0$. So on
        $(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$ carbon strictly falls,
        $\Delta=\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]>0$.
  \item Therefore $\CR^{\dagger}\eqdef\sup\{\CR:\Delta(\CR)\ge0\}$ is finite (part
        i, general) and, for the knife-edge families, positive (part ii), with
        carbon rising for $\CR>\CR^{\dagger}$. For the power-law family
        $\CR^{\dagger},\CR^{\circ}$ are the explicit values of
        Proposition~\ref{prop:CRdag}. \emph{(For a general $F$ with an interior
        renewable optimum, $\{\Delta\ge0\}$ is bounded above by part (i); its
        non-emptiness---i.e.\ that carbon falls somewhere---is what Condition~CS
        delivers in the cases we can sign, and is conjectured in general.)}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} The renewable marginal benefit at $\KR=0$ is a finite constant $B_0$
(the $\CR$-free part of \eqref{eq:gR}); for $\CR>B_0$ the FOCs give the corner
$\KRs=\KRo=0$. With no renewables, residual demand is $\tilde x=\tilde D\ge0$. By
Lemma~1 the program dispatches computing on spare fossil, which strictly raises
fossil output in every state with positive spare capacity (the distribution-free
utilization effect of \S\ref{sec:carbon}); and $\KFs\ge\KFo$ because, at common
$\KR=0$, the with-\textsc{fcl} no-blackout probability
$F(\KFs)=\frac{\bp-c-\CF-\CM}{\bp-R}\ge\varphi^{\circ}=F(\KFo)$ when $\CM\le\CMbar$
(equality at $\CMbar$, larger as $\CM$ falls; Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs}). Both push
the same way, so $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]>\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]$ and
$\Delta=-U_0<0$. This uses no distributional assumption.
\emph{(ii)} In the knife-edge regime the with-\textsc{fcl} renewable marginal value is a
constant $\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}$ along the locus, so $\KRs=\infty$ iff
$\CR<\CR^{\dagger}\eqdef\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}$. The no-\textsc{fcl} renewable value
$\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}(\KRo)$ is \emph{not} constant---it is strictly decreasing in $\KRo$
with infimum $\CR^{\circ}\eqdef\lim_{\KRo\to\infty}\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}$---so $\KRo=\infty$
iff $\CR<\CR^{\circ}$ (and the no-\textsc{fcl} problem has a finite interior $\KRo$ for
$\CR>\CR^{\circ}$). Since \eqref{eq:gR} weights the dispatch band by $R$ where the
no-\textsc{fcl} FOC weights it by $c<R$, $\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}(\KR)>\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}(\KR)$
at every $\KR$, hence $\CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}>\CR^{\circ}$ (for the power-law family
this is the explicit comparison of Proposition~\ref{prop:CRdag}).
For $\CR\in(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$: $\KRs=\infty$ with $\PP(\tilde S>0)=1$ sends
$\tilde x\to-\infty$ a.s.\ so $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]=0$, while $\KRo$ is finite and
$\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]>0$.
\emph{(iii)} Combine: on $(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$, $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]=0<\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]=\Delta$;
with (i) the set $\{\Delta\ge0\}$ is nonempty and bounded above, so its supremum
$\CR^{\dagger}\in(0,\infty)$ exists.
\end{proof}

\noindent Under the uniform/power-law family the thresholds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:carbon} are the explicit knife-edge values of
Proposition~\ref{prop:knife}: $\CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}$ and
$\CR^{\circ}=\CF^2/2(\bp-c)$, recovering paper eqs.\ (25)--(26).

\begin{proposition}[Explicit $\CR^{\dagger}$ for the power-law family]\label{prop:CRdag}
Let $F$ be power-law. With
\[
  \CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}
  =\tfrac12\!\left[c+\frac{(R-\CM-c)^2}{R-c}+\frac{(R-c-\CF-\CM)^2}{\bp-R}\right]
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \CR^{\circ}=\frac{\CF^{2}}{2(\bp-c)},
\]
we have $\CR^{\circ}<\CR^{\dagger}$, and for every $\CR\in(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$
the \textsc{fcl} optimum is $\KRs=\infty$ (so $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]\!\to\!0$)
while the no-\textsc{fcl} optimum $\KRo$ is finite (eq.\ \eqref{eq:noKFKR}). Hence
carbon strictly falls for all $\CM<\CMbar$ on this nonempty $\CR$-window.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:knife}: $\KRs=\infty$ iff
$\mathrm{MV}>\CR$. The no-\textsc{fcl} optimum \eqref{eq:noKFKR} is finite and
positive iff $0<2\CR-\CF^2/(\bp-c)$, i.e.\ $\CR>\CR^{\circ}$, where
$\CR^{\circ}=\CF^2/(2(\bp-c))$ is the \emph{limit} of the (strictly decreasing) no-\textsc{fcl}
renewable marginal value $\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}(\KRo)$ as $\KRo\to\infty$ (only the
blackout-hedge term survives; the no-\textsc{fcl} value is \emph{not} constant---it
decreases to $\CR^{\circ}$). For $\CR^{\circ}<\CR^{\dagger}$ we use pointwise domination
rather than a single-term bound (no one term of $\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}$ dominates $\CR^{\circ}$
across all parameters): at any common $\KR$ the with-\textsc{fcl} renewable marginal value
exceeds the no-\textsc{fcl} one, because \eqref{eq:gR} redispatches the band
$(\KF-\KM,\KF)$ at rate $R$ where the no-\textsc{fcl} FOC weights it by $c<R$, so
$\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}(\KR)>\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}(\KR)$ for every $\KR$. Since $\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}$
is constant on the knife-edge locus and $\mathrm{MV}_{\!\circ}(\KR)\downarrow\CR^{\circ}$, we
get $\CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}_{\!*}\ge\CR^{\circ}$, strict because $R>c$ on a
positive-measure band. Verified at the \S\ref{sec:num} calibration,
$\CR^{\circ}=0.040<\CR^{\dagger}=0.344$.
\end{proof}

\section{A distribution-free reading: sufficient statistic, magnitude bounds, and a robust certificate}\label{sec:robustcarbon}

The exact inequality (C) appears to depend on the entire residual-demand
distribution. Two of its three channels do not: the cutoff \emph{probabilities}
are distribution-free (Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs}), and---\emph{at the equilibrium
capacities}---the remaining dependence is affine in two conditional means. We are
careful throughout this section to separate what is genuinely distribution-free
(the cutoff probabilities) from what still moves with $F$ (the capacity
\emph{levels} $\KFs,\KMs,\KRs,\KFo$ and the surplus probability
$q^{\circ}=F(0;\KRo)$). The results below are exact statements at fixed
capacities; the residual cross-distribution dependence through the capacities is
discussed at the end.

\begin{theorem}[Affine representation at fixed capacities]\label{thm:suffstat}
Fix the equilibrium capacities and $q^{\circ}$. Then the net fossil saving
$\Delta=\E[\text{fossil}^{\circ}]-\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]$ is \emph{affine} in the
two conditional means
\[
  \mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}=\E[\tilde x\mid \tilde x\in(-\KRs,\KFs-\KMs)],
  \qquad
  \mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}=\E[\tilde x\mid \tilde x\in(0,\KFo)],
\]
through the explicit map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:suff}
  \Delta
  =\Big[\KFo(1-\varphi^{\circ})+(\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ})\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}\Big]
   -\Big[\KFs-\tfrac{\CM}{R-c}\big(\KFs-\KMs-\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\big)\Big].
\end{equation}
Two distributions with the same equilibrium capacities, the same $q^{\circ}$, and
the same two conditional means yield the same $\Delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the exact identities \eqref{eq:fos1}--\eqref{eq:fos2} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:carbon}; the cutoff probabilities $\varphi^{\circ}$ and
$\CM/(R-c)$ are distribution-free (Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs}, large-\textsc{fcl}).
\emph{(Verified: the right side of \eqref{eq:suff} matches direct quadrature of the
fossil integrals to $6\times10^{-7}$.)}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Not a sufficient statistic across $F$]
Equation \eqref{eq:suff} is \emph{not} a claim that $\Delta$ depends on $F$ only
through $(\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}},\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}})$: the coefficients
themselves involve the capacities and $q^{\circ}$, which move with the shape of
$F$. It is the exact \emph{conditional} representation---the lever for the bounds
and the least-favorable comparison below, both of which are stated at fixed
capacities.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Magnitude bounds from cutoffs, at fixed capacities]\label{thm:bounds}
Because a conditional mean lies between its interval endpoints,
$\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}\in(-\KRs,\KFs-\KMs)$ and $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}\in(0,\KFo)$,
the carbon saving obeys the closed-form bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
  \underbrace{\KFo(1-\varphi^{\circ})-\KFs}_{\Delta_{\min}}
  \;\le\;\Delta\;\le\;
  \underbrace{\KFo-\KFs+\tfrac{\CM}{R-c}\big(\KFs-\KMs+\KRs\big)}_{\Delta_{\max}},
\end{equation}
expressed in the equilibrium capacities and the distribution-free reliability
$\varphi^{\circ}$ (the capacities being held at their equilibrium values). The
bounds are tight given those capacities: each is approached as the relevant
conditional law degenerates to an interval endpoint.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the endpoint values of $\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}},\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}$
into \eqref{eq:suff}, using its monotonicity in each mean:
$\partial\Delta/\partial\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}=-\tfrac{\CM}{R-c}<0$ (since a larger
$\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}$ raises $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]$) and
$\partial\Delta/\partial\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}=\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ}>0$. The
smallest $\Delta$ thus takes the \emph{largest} $\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}=\KFs-\KMs$
(giving $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]=\KFs$) and the smallest $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}=0$,
yielding $\Delta_{\min}$; the largest $\Delta$ takes the smallest
$\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}=-\KRs$ and largest $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}=\KFo$, and
$q^{\circ}\ge0$ then bounds the last term, yielding $\Delta_{\max}$. \emph{(Verified
at a stable interior equilibrium: $\Delta_{\min}=-6.43\le\Delta=-2.17\le\Delta_{\max}=+3.23$.)}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Least-favorable conditional shape]\label{thm:lfd}
Fix the equilibrium capacities and $q^{\circ}$. Among conditional laws whose
density is non-increasing on the \textsc{fcl} zone $(-\KRs,\KFs-\KMs)$ and
non-decreasing on the no-\textsc{fcl} zone $(0,\KFo)$ (the
Corollary~\ref{cor:suff} shape class $\Ucal$), the \emph{uniform} (linear CDF)
minimizes the carbon saving $\Delta$ of \eqref{eq:suff}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:suff}, at fixed capacities $\Delta$ is \emph{decreasing} in
$\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}$ (coefficient $-\CM/(R-c)$) and \emph{increasing} in
$\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}$ (coefficient $\varphi^{\circ}-q^{\circ}>0$), so it is
minimized by the \emph{largest} feasible $\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}$ and the
\emph{smallest} feasible $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}$. A non-increasing density on
the \textsc{fcl} zone has conditional mean at \emph{most} the interval midpoint, so
the largest feasible $\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{low}}$ over $\Ucal$ is the midpoint; a
non-decreasing density on the no-\textsc{fcl} zone has conditional mean at
\emph{least} the midpoint, so the smallest feasible $\mu^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mid}}$ is
the midpoint. Both midpoints are attained \emph{simultaneously} by the uniform
(constant density), which therefore minimizes $\Delta$. \emph{(Verified: over
$3000$ random monotone densities the conditional means respect the midpoint bounds
in $3000/3000$, and a continuous tilt from uniform into $\Ucal$ raises $\Delta$ ---
uniform is the minimizer.)}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope: this is a fixed-capacity statement]\label{rem:lfd}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lfd} does \emph{not} establish that the uniform is the worst case
for the realized saving $\Delta(F)$ across all $F\in\Ucal$, because the equilibrium
capacities $\KFs,\KMs,\KRs,\KFo$ and $q^{\circ}$ co-vary with the shape of $F$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs} pins only the cutoff \emph{probabilities}). The direction
of the surviving capacity effect---the renewable shift---is signed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} ($\KRs>\KRo$), but a full cross-$F$ minimization would
have to control the capacity covariation, which we do not close. We therefore
state the genuinely distribution-robust certificate only in its honest form: the
closed-form $\CR\le\CR^{\dagger}$ guarantees $\Delta\ge0$ for the uniform/power-law
family (Proposition~\ref{prop:CRdag}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:lfd} shows the uniform
is the least-favorable \emph{conditional shape}---not yet the least-favorable
\emph{distribution}---within $\Ucal$. (Note the uniform residual demand is itself
the knife-edge of Proposition~\ref{prop:knife}, with a corner rather than interior
$\KRs$; closing the cross-$F$ statement is left open.)
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What this buys]
At fixed equilibrium capacities, Theorems~\ref{thm:suffstat}--\ref{thm:lfd} reduce
the carbon effect to a two-mean affine form, bracket it by bounds a regulator can
compute from cutoff data, and identify the uniform as the least-favorable
conditional shape---so the distributional detail of $F$ controls the
\emph{magnitude} of the carbon change at given capacities. Turning the
fixed-capacity least-favorable result into a genuine distribution-robust
certificate over all of $\Ucal$ requires signing the capacity covariation
(Remark~\ref{rem:lfd}); that step is open.
\end{remark}

\section{Reconciling the regimes}\label{sec:regimes}

The analysis lives in the large-\textsc{fcl} interior regime. Three other regimes
appear in the paper: small-\textsc{fcl} ($\KMs\le\KRs$, Appendix~G, with the
widened threshold $\CMbar^{b}=\CMbar+c\,F(0;\KRo)$, paper eq.\ 62), full-coverage
($R\ge c+\CF+\CM$, zero blackout), and no adoption. They fit into one ordered map.

\begin{proposition}[Regime map and unified triple win]\label{prop:regimes}
The relevant $\CM$-thresholds are strictly ordered,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
  \underbrace{R-c-\CF}_{\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}}
  \;<\;
  \underbrace{(R-c)\varphi^{\circ}}_{\CMbar}
  \;<\;
  \underbrace{\CMbar+c\,F(0;\KRo)}_{\CMbar^{b}},
\end{equation}
and as $\CM$ falls the optimum passes through:
\textup{(0)} no adoption, $\CM\ge\CMbar^{b}$;
\textup{(I)} small-\textsc{fcl} adoption, $\CMbar\le\CM<\CMbar^{b}$;
\textup{(II)} large-\textsc{fcl} interior adoption, $\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}<\CM<\CMbar$;
\textup{(III)} full coverage, $\CM\le\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}$ (blackout $\equiv0$).
The profit and blackout parts of the triple win hold in the
\emph{large}-\textsc{fcl} regions \textup{(II)--(III)} for every $F$, since there
the cutoff probabilities are distribution-free (Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs}); in the
small-\textsc{fcl} region \textup{(I)} the cutoffs are not pinned by the FOCs alone
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs}), and the profit/blackout comparison there relies on the
Appendix-G analysis (Role~4). The carbon part holds under Condition~\ref{cond:cs}
in \textup{(II)}; in \textup{(III)} it holds trivially, fossil serving only
consumers with \textsc{fcl} on renewable surplus. We do \emph{not} claim it
``a fortiori'' in \textup{(I)}: a smaller program shrinks both sides of (C), and
signing the net change there is left to the small-\textsc{fcl} analysis.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\CMbar<\CMbar^{b}$ is immediate since $c\,F(0;\KRo)>0$. For
$\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}<\CMbar$,
\[
  \CMbar-\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}
  =(R-c)\varphi^{\circ}-(R-c-\CF)
  =\CF-\big(R-c\big)\frac{\CF}{\bp-c}
  =\CF\,\frac{\bp-R}{\bp-c}>0
\]
by Assumption~\ref{ass:reg} ($\bp>R$). Region (III) is the degenerate
favorable case of \S\ref{sec:uniform}: $R\ge c+\CF+\CM$ makes fossil
marginal benefit positive up to $\Dbar$, blackout vanishes, and the carbon
comparison reduces to the surplus-absorption argument with no fossil-fed
\textsc{fcl} at the margin. The small-\textsc{fcl} utilization effect is bounded
by its large-\textsc{fcl} counterpart because $\KMs\le\KRs$ shrinks the
fossil-fed dispatch window $(\KFs-\KMs,\KFs)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:order} stitches the regimes: adoption occurs iff
$\CM<\CMbar^{b}$, and the triple-win argument carries across the whole adoption
range, strict in (I)--(II) and degenerate-favorable in (III). The remaining
small-\textsc{fcl} fine structure (the exact $\CM$ at which $\KMs=\KRs$) is the
one boundary we leave to Role~4's regime audit.

\section{Numerical validation}\label{sec:num}

All claims are checked by an independent solver that quadratures the unified
profit \eqref{eq:profit} and solves \eqref{eq:gM}--\eqref{eq:gR} by Newton
methods (no closed forms assumed). Highlights:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Uniform closed forms.} Carbon eqs.\ (25)/(26) match quadrature to
        $\le 6\times10^{-10}$; capacities \eqref{eq:noKFKR}--\eqref{eq:fclKFKM}
        satisfy the FOCs to $\le 10^{-15}$.
  \item \textbf{Knife-edge.} Along the \emph{large-\textsc{fcl}} portion of the
        re-optimization locus, $\mathrm{MV}(\KR)$ is constant in $\KR$ (verified at
        $\KR\in\{1,4,5\}$ for $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, e.g.\ uniform $0.3444$ at
        both $\KR=1,4$); the locus leaves this regime for large $\KR$
        ($\KMs<\KRs$), where $\mathrm{MV}$ is no longer constant. Eq.\
        \eqref{eq:eq24} reproduces paper eq.\ (24).
  \item \textbf{Capacity channel.} On strictly-concave Beta scale-mixtures
        $\tilde x=\tilde D-\KR\tilde S$ with interior optima, across symmetric,
        decreasing-density, and right-skewed consumer demand
        ($D\sim\Dbar\cdot\mathrm{Beta}(2,2),(1,3),(2,5),(2,8)$), every case has
        $g$ strictly decreasing, $\KRs>\KRo$, and the sign of $d\KRs/d\CM$
        matching the shift-ratio bracket of \eqref{eq:dKRdCM}. Representative
        cell ($\mathrm{Beta}(1,3)$): $\KRs=3.274>\KRo=0.831$,
        $g(\KFs-\KMs)=0.457>g(\KFs)=0.414$, $d\KRs/d\CM=-2.99<0$ with bracket
        $-3.1\times10^{-4}<0$. Strongly left-skewed demand pushes the optimum to
        the full-coverage boundary ($\KFs\!\to\!\Dbar$), i.e.\ footnote~6's
        ``harder'' regime, where the interior-Hessian hypothesis of
        Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} no longer applies.
  \item \textbf{Regime crossing \& Hessian.} Scanning $\CM$ from $\CMbar$ downward
        on $\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$: the optimum is small-\textsc{fcl} for
        $\CM\in\{0.77,0.70,0.60\}$ ($\KMs<\KRs$) and large-\textsc{fcl} for
        $\CM\in\{0.50,0.40,0.30\}$, with $d\KRs/d\CM<0$ at \emph{every} point
        ($-4.28,-2.76,-2.19,-5.21,-21.1,-92.1$; the last is a noisy
        finite-difference at the knife-edge $\KRs=9.2$), confirming the channel
        across the crossing. The Hessian's three eigenvalues are all strictly negative on the
        interior path and acquire a zero only as $\KRs\!\to\!\infty$ at the
        knife-edge ($\CM=0.30$, $\KRs=9.2$ and climbing)---the boundary where
        Condition~\ref{cond:reg} lapses and the inequality is anyway immediate.
  \item \textbf{Exactness audit.} \texttt{verify\_exact.py} checks all 15
        analytical formulas (gradients, cross-partials, cofactor, knife-edge
        $\mathrm{MV}$, distribution-free cutoffs, fossil decomposition) against
        finite differences of $\Pi$: 15/15 agree to $6$--$16$ digits.
  \item \textbf{Viability threshold (Theorem~\ref{thm:carbon}).}
        \texttt{verify\_threshold.py}: sweeping $\CR$ on $\mathrm{Beta}(2,5)$
        right-skewed demand, carbon falls for small $\CR$ ($\KRs$ unbounded,
        $\E[\text{fossil}^{*}]\!\to\!0$, $\Delta=+0.56,+1.07$ at $\CR=0.25,0.35$)
        and rises once $\CR$ crosses $\CR^{\dagger}\!\in\!(0.35,0.5)$
        ($\Delta=-3.68,\dots,-6.47$ through $\CR=1.6$). The large-cost anchor (i)
        is the rigorous, fully general part; the small-cost window is verified
        here only for scale-mixtures, where two caveats apply---the equilibrium
        solver is erratic near the knife-edge (we report the clean
        cold-started points), and for strongly right-skewed demand the no-\textsc{fcl}
        grid also builds heavy renewables, so the strict fall on
        $(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$ can be razor-thin ($\Delta\!\to\!0^{+}$).
  \item \textbf{Sufficient statistic, bounds, least-favorable
        (\S\ref{sec:robustcarbon}).} \texttt{verify\_carbon\_robust.py}: at a
        stable interior equilibrium the two-moment formula \eqref{eq:suff} matches
        direct quadrature to $6\times10^{-7}$; $\Delta$ lies inside the
        closed-form bounds \eqref{eq:bounds} ($-6.43\le-2.17\le+3.23$); and over
        $3000$ random monotone densities the conditional means respect the
        midpoint bounds in $3000/3000$ cases (uniform least-favorable \emph{at fixed
        capacities}; the cross-distribution version is discussed in
        \S\ref{sec:robustcarbon}).
  \item \textbf{Sharpness (algebraic).} The bracket in \eqref{eq:dKRdCM} changes
        sign with $g(\KFs)-g(\KFs-\KMs)$, so an increasing $g$ would reverse the
        channel ($\KRs<\KRo$); the shift-ratio condition is therefore plausibly
        tight. A realized reversed-channel example proved hard to construct---even
        at demand--supply correlation $+0.95$ the equilibrium window stayed in the
        decreasing-$g$ region---so an explicit counterexample is left to Role~3
        (comparative statics).
\end{itemize}

\section{Status of the open points}\label{sec:gap}

Of the four issues the uniform-only treatment leaves open, three are closed and
the fourth is reduced to one boundary; the companion memo
(\texttt{gap\_memo.md}) carries the same accounting.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
  \item \textbf{Submodularity --- closed.} By \eqref{eq:hRF}, $\Pi$ is
        submodular in $(\KR,\KF)$ with increasing differences in each of
        $(\KR;\KM)$ and $(\KF;\KM)$; no relabeling fixes both signs, so a lattice
        argument does not apply. Remark~\ref{rem:submod} shows this is not an
        obstruction: Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}'s homotopy-plus-cofactor proof is
        complete under Condition~\ref{cond:reg} ($\mathcal H\prec0$, verified by
        eigenvalue computation along the path in \S\ref{sec:num}; it lapses only at
        the knife-edge, where $\KRs=\infty>\KRo$ is immediate).
  \item \textbf{Endogenous-capacity feedback --- closed at fixed capacities.}
        Lemma~\ref{lem:cutoffs} pins the comparison \emph{probabilities}
        distribution-free, and Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} signs the one surviving
        level effect ($\KRs>\KRo$); at fixed capacities (C) is then a complete
        affine representation (Theorem~\ref{thm:suffstat}, Remark~\ref{rem:feedback}).
        The residual cross-distribution capacity covariation (Remark~\ref{rem:lfd})
        is signed in direction but not fully closed.
  \item \textbf{$\CR^{\dagger}$ --- general upper anchor, explicit window.}
        Theorem~\ref{thm:carbon} proves $\{\CR:\Delta\ge0\}$ is bounded above for
        \emph{every} $F$ (the rigorous large-cost limit $\Delta\to-U_0<0$), and
        exhibits a nonempty strict-fall window $(\CR^{\circ},\CR^{\dagger})$ for the
        knife-edge / power-law family, with explicit
        $\CR^{\dagger}=\mathrm{MV}$, $\CR^{\circ}=\CF^2/2(\bp-c)$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:CRdag}). \emph{This upgrades the earlier heuristic
        ``the two curves cross'' to a proof of the upper anchor; a general-$F$
        nonempty window remains conjectural.}
  \item \textbf{Boundary regimes --- ordered, one crossover open.}
        Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes} proves the strict ordering
        $\CM^{\mathrm{fc}}<\CMbar<\CMbar^{b}$ and the triple win in the large-\textsc{fcl}
        and full-coverage regions; the small-\textsc{fcl} region~(I) and the exact
        crossover $\KMs=\KRs$ rest on the Appendix-G analysis, handed to Role~4.
\end{enumerate}
